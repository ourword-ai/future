An AI receptionist that answers a solo business's phone after hours…

An AI receptionist that answers a solo business's phone after hours just cleared 129 upvotes on Product Hunt in English. The Chinese single-shop equivalent cannot be a translation, because the customer arrives on WeChat, not a phone line.

Updated 2026-08-03 · Source https://github.com/pppscn/SmsForwarder · 中文版

What is the idea?

The Western pattern — Estera and peers — answers a business's phone and WhatsApp around the clock, books the appointment, and hands the owner a summary. It assumes the customer dials a number.

Who would use it, and what do they get?

One person who owns one shop and answers her own WeChat — a hair-salon chair, a car-repair bay, a pet groomer, a single dental chair. What she gets is not a dashboard: it is a three-minute morning list of the enquiries that arrived after she pulled the shutter down, ranked by who is most likely to come in today, each with one sentence already drafted that she can send herself.

Why would anyone bother?

A hair salon, a car-repair bay, a pet groomer or a single dental chair loses the enquiry outright when the hands are occupied or the shutter is down. The enquiry does not wait; it goes to the shop next door.

What is missing today?

The Chinese-market case is empty and it is not a translation job: the customer does not call, they message a WeChat personal account or a group. Every Western product here is built around a phone number and a calendar API — neither is where Chinese single-shop demand actually lands.

What makes this one different?

Do not automate the WeChat account — that is the trap and this board's own integrity line would veto it. Sit beside it: the owner forwards or screenshots after-hours enquiries into one place, and next morning gets a ranked list of who asked what with a drafted reply per person, which they send themselves. One job, no platform violation, no calendar integration. Cold start (Chinese): Xiaohongshu and Douyin comment sections under 单人理发店 and 宠物店 日常 posts, where owners describe losing customers; plus the SmsForwarder issue tracker, where users are already asking for native WeChat and QQ channels. Cold start (English): r/smallbusiness, search 'missed calls after hours solo owner'.

What is the sharpest thing about it?

It turns the most expensive failure mode of a one-person shop — the phone ringing while both hands are busy — into a booked appointment, with nobody on payroll.

What could kill it?

WeChat's rules are hostile to automating a personal account, which is very likely why nobody has shipped it — this may be a rules problem wearing the costume of a product problem. Meituan and Dianping also already own booking in the largest verticals.

Who pays, and for what?

Monthly subscription from a single-shop owner, priced against one lost customer a month.

What is the main risk?

If it drifts into automating the WeChat account itself it becomes platform-parasitic — banned by the platform and vetoed here.

What did real people actually say?

Verbatim from the public sources linked above, not paraphrased: “现在QQ和微信都原生支持机器人了,支持一下吧,这样方便很多,不需要为了通知专门装一个企业微信了” — github-issue

How much work is it?

about two months of work
