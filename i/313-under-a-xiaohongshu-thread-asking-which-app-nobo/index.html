Under a Xiaohongshu thread asking which app nobody has built (761…

Under a Xiaohongshu thread asking which app nobody has built (761 likes, 1,130 saves, 214 comments), the most specific repeated request is not calorie counting — it is food logging read against your own medical check-up report, and commenters say the existing apps cover too little and nobody has the standing to build it.

Updated 2026-08-04 · Source https://www.xiaohongshu.com/explore/69781bb7000000000903ae66 · 中文版

What is the idea?

Nothing yet — this is the shape of the hole. What people are asking for: point the phone at a nutrition label, and get a verdict that knows what their own bloodwork said. Not a calorie diary; a buy / do-not-buy / swap-this call, grounded in the report they already paid for.

Who would use it, and what do they get?

Non-developer user: an ordinary adult who has just had a company or annual physical. Finished thing: a one-line buy/skip verdict on the packet in their hand, tied to their own numbers.

Why would anyone bother?

The report says 'triglycerides high'. The aisle says 'contains vegetable oil, maltose syrup, 12g sugar per 100g'. Nobody translates between those two, so the report changes nothing.

What is missing today?

It hits a real pain and every existing product solves the wrong half. 薄荷 / Cal AI / Caloris are calorie diaries — you log what you already ate. Nobody reads the packet before you buy it, and nobody connects either end to the check-up report. Commenters name the blocker themselves: they assume you need hospital data access, which is exactly the assumption that keeps the aisle-side version — which needs none — unbuilt.

What makes this one different?

Refuse the hospital-integration fantasy that everyone in that comment thread assumes is required. The user uploads the PDF or photo of the report they already hold, three to five flagged lines get turned into three to five plain-language shopping rules, and from then on the product does exactly one thing: photograph a nutrition label, get a one-line verdict against those rules plus one alternative on the same shelf. No diet plan, no diagnosis, no meal logging, and every verdict ends with what to ask a doctor or dietitian. Cold start (Chinese): the comment tree of this note and 「配料表 怎么看」 / 「体检报告 解读」 on Xiaohongshu, where people are already posting label photos and asking strangers; Cold start (English): r/nutrition and r/loseit, search 'which one should I buy' and 'read my bloodwork'.

What is the sharpest thing about it?

It converts a document people already receive once a year and then file away into a decision they make forty times a week.

What could kill it?

This is health-adjacent, so the honest ceiling is low: you can translate and compare, you cannot advise, and the moment it says 'do not eat this' it is practising medicine. Chinese platforms are also circling — a big check-up chain or an insurer can bundle it, and 薄荷 can add label OCR in one release. The deeper risk is behavioural: people who read labels already read labels, and people who do not will not start because an app asked them to.

Who pays, and for what?

Someone who just got a report with red lines on it — the highest-intent moment there is. One-off unlock or a yearly fee timed to the annual check-up, not a subscription to a food diary.

What is the main risk?

The self-imposed ceiling (translate and compare, never advise) is also the reason it may feel too thin to pay for.

What did real people actually say?

Verbatim from the public sources linked above, not paraphrased: “要输入自己吃的食物、习惯分析缺许多维生素、各类营养物质和热量的，还可以接入体检报告，fit那个功能太有限了。这种软件需要专业的医院和医生以及大量数据来做，可惜没人做。” — xiaohongshu “有没有可以拍照食品营养成分表，自动文字识别录入信息，进行汇总和比对的” — xiaohongshu

How much work is it?

about two weeks of work
