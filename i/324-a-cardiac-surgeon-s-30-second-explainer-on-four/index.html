A cardiac surgeon's 30-second explainer on four blood-test numbers…

A cardiac surgeon's 30-second explainer on four blood-test numbers has 16k likes, 35k saves and 194 comments on Xiaohongshu — and the comments are not thanks. They are ordinary people pasting their own values and asking a stranger to read them. Under the same query what ranks is a how-to for getting an LLM to read your report, not a product.

Updated 2026-08-06 · Source https://www.xiaohongshu.com/explore/6a685e3700000000050393a7 · 中文版

What is the idea?

Photograph your check-up report. Get back one thing — this week's grocery list, plus a swap-one-item-a-day table — instead of another explanation of what LDL means.

Who would use it, and what do they get?

Someone holding a check-up report with three numbers in red. What they get is this week's shopping list and the one thing to swap out each day.

Why would anyone bother?

A person holding a printout with three numbers in red, who does not have a doctor to ask and will not book an appointment over a borderline lipid panel. What they walk away with is a shopping list they can use tonight — not a paragraph of explanation they will close and forget.

What is missing today?

The two sides never meet. Check-up products stop at translating the report into plain language; diet-logging products count calories and have never seen your lab sheet. The most-repeated request in Chinese comment sections is precisely the join — log what I eat and read it against my check-up — and the consensus about the incumbents (薄荷 / Cal AI / Caloris) is that they cover too little.

What makes this one different?

Do not build a health app. Build one artefact from one document: lipid panel plus fasting glucose in, a week's grocery list and a one-swap-a-day table out, with 'this is not a diagnosis — take abnormal values to a doctor' fixed at the bottom. No wearables, no long-term tracking, no medication. Cold start (Chinese): the 194 comments under that one note are already a list of named people asking, plus the 「体检报告 看不懂」 and 「怎么让AI解读体检报告」 queries; (English): r/Biohackers and r/nutrition threads where someone posts bloodwork and asks what to eat differently.

What is the sharpest thing about it?

Reading a Chinese hospital check-up sheet (dozens of labs, different reference ranges per hospital, scanned or photographed) into structured values is a multimodal task that only became reliable recently. The people in that comment section have already noticed: the top-ranking posts under this query are step-by-step guides to pasting your report into DeepSeek, which is the tell that the capability arrived and the product did not.

What could kill it?

This is health advice with a thin line under it, and the line is where the value is: the moment it says 'eat this' it is one step from dosing supplements and two from telling someone to skip a doctor. The honest version is deliberately smaller than the exciting version. Chinese super-apps and the check-up chains themselves already sit on the report at the moment of delivery, and diet-logging retention is famously awful — people log for eleven days.

Who pays, and for what?

An adult who just did a company annual check-up and has three values in red — one payment per report, at the price of a takeaway meal, at the one moment per year they care.

What is the main risk?

Medical scope creep is the failure mode, and the check-up chains hold the report first; diet logging has a retention floor that no amount of AI has yet lifted.
