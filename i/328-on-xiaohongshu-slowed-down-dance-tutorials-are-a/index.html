On Xiaohongshu, slowed-down dance tutorials are a whole content…

On Xiaohongshu, slowed-down dance tutorials are a whole content category — 6,909 likes, 4,667, 2,979, 2,727 — and every single one exists only because a teacher was willing to re-dance that song by hand. The commenters say exactly why: at that speed, they believe they can learn it. There is a Chinese word for the labour behind this (扒舞) and one of the platform's own search suggestions is 扒舞好累啊 — 'reverse-engineering choreography is exhausting'.

Updated 2026-08-08 · Source https://www.xiaohongshu.com/explore/69fc99d50000000038034a67 · 中文版

What is the idea?

Drop in one dance video you found yourself, and 30 seconds later get the version you can actually practise to: segmented into eight-counts along the music, each segment at half speed, one plain-language cue per count ('left hand to chest, right foot points forward'), and a printable move list. Version two opens the camera and tells you which eight-count you got wrong.

Who would use it, and what do they get?

Someone learning a dance alone from a phone, with no teacher and no studio; the deliverable is that specific dance cut into eight-counts, slowed, and narrated one count at a time.

Why would anyone bother?

Because whether an ordinary person can learn a dance at all is decided by speed and segmentation, and today that depends entirely on whether some teacher happened to hand-produce a slow version of that specific song. If nobody did, you stop.

What is missing today?

The existing 扒舞 apps are speed / mirror / frame-step / loop players — 终于有工具可以逐帧练舞了 (354 likes), 小众APP｜跳舞人必备扒舞APP (412), plus individuals building their own (习舞一年，我做了一个新手练舞专用 App). Not one of them understands what is happening in the frame: no automatic eight-count splitting, no cues, no feedback on your own body, and none of them can produce the back-facing view everybody actually wants. Mirroring is one line of code and was never the hard part — mirroring flips the clothes, the room and the face too, which is precisely why teachers film a separate back-view take.

What makes this one different?

Do not build another player — build the thing a teacher does by hand: cut to the eight-count, slow it, and say out loud what the body does on each count, with every cue clickable back to its own frame and left blank rather than guessed when the model is unsure. Local-only on a video the user already found; never host, never redistribute. Cold start (Chinese): the comment sections under the slow-tutorial posts themselves (6,909 / 4,667 / 2,979 / 2,727 likes) are full of people asking for a slow version of one specific song, plus the notes that land on the searches 扒舞好累啊 and 什么app比较适合扒舞. Cold start (English): the 'mirrored version please' comments under YouTube dance tutorials, and r/kpophelp.

What is the sharpest thing about it?

Every tool in this space is a video player. This one reads the movement — which is what makes eight-count segmentation, spoken cues and error-spotting possible at all.

What could kill it?

Douyin and Kuaishou sit on both the videos and the pose models and could ship speed + segmentation as a player feature. Cue generation is the real risk: say 'left' when it is right and you are worse than useless, because this audience trains by muscle memory and a wrong move takes longer to unlearn than to learn. And back-view synthesis from a single camera is still research, so version one must not promise it.

Who pays, and for what?

People learning a dance alone from a phone — from K-pop covers to wedding first dances to square dancing. They pay per dance broken down, or a small subscription once they are learning one a month. The teachers who currently hand-make slow versions are the second market: it collapses a day of work into minutes.

What is the main risk?

Cue accuracy. A wrong left/right does more damage than no cue at all.
